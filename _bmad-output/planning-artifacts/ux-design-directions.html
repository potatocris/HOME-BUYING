What works

Result is unmissable — headline is the first thing the eye lands on. Scenario comparison is immediately below. Inputs are out of the way until needed. Closely mirrors NYT structure.

Trade-offs

User must scroll to reach inputs. First-time users may not discover the expanders. Expander section headers need to be obviously clickable.

Best for

Users who trust the Miami defaults and want the answer first. Returning users who know the tool. Shared-link recipients who just want to see the result.

What works

Inputs and outputs always visible simultaneously — no scrolling, no expanders. Ideal for power users exploring many assumptions. Eliminates the need to scroll down to find sliders.

Trade-offs

Streamlit's sidebar is a different context from the main area — feels like navigation, not input. Requires careful implementation to avoid the sidebar feeling like a settings menu rather than a calculation panel.

Best for

Users who will adjust many inputs in sequence. Cris in "deep analysis" mode. Less ideal for first-time or shared-link visits where the output should dominate.

What works

Familiar mental model — "fill in the form, then see the result." Lower learning curve for users coming from spreadsheets. Inputs are prominent and feel intentional rather than secondary.

Trade-offs

Headline is below the fold — shared-link recipients must scroll to see the result. Goes against the "results first" experience principle. Weakens the tool's core differentiator (instant answer on load).

Best for

Users who feel anxious about trusting defaults and want to verify inputs before seeing results. Less aligned with the project's core experience principles.
